Riemannian geometry and Euclidean geometry are two different types of geometries that measure distances in different ways. In Euclidean geometry, distances are measured between points that are equal in all directions. In Riemannian geometry, however, distances are measured along the geodesics of a surface. Furthermore, Euclidean geometry is based on the notion of parallel lines, while Riemannian geometry is based on the notion of parallel transport. Finally, Euclidean spaces have an infinite number of dimensions, while Riemannian spaces have a finite number of dimensions. These are the main differences between Euclidean geometry and Riemannian geometry.
