## Consistency, which is not validity

- swap: 1170 pair(s) judged both ways, 867 decided both times, 123 flipped — flip rate 0.142 under `decided-only`, 95% cluster-jackknife CI [0.112, 0.171]. kappa 0.579 under `abstain-as-category` (p_o 0.731, p_e 0.360, PABAK 0.596, 1170 row(s) over 195 item(s)), 95% cluster-jackknife CI [0.528, 0.631].
- re-run: 200 comparison(s) across 2 seed(s). kappa 0.795 under `abstain-as-category` (p_o 0.860, p_e 0.316, PABAK 0.813), 95% cluster-jackknife CI [0.725, 0.865].

Both are the judge against itself. The two readings are not independent,
which pushes observed agreement up, so neither is a two-rater reliability
and neither is evidence that the judge is measuring the right thing.

The re-run arm moves no candidate. Both orders of every pair are asked at
every seed, so a second seed reorders nothing; what it moves is the nonce
inside the candidate fences. The re-run coefficient is therefore the same
decision under an irrelevant-token perturbation, together with whatever the
server does differently at temperature 0, and it does not separate the two.

The replay checks itself where the two rules must agree. On the 179 run(s) the new rule settled with a Condorcet winner and no consensus, the old rule required the same sweep of the same six answers and had to name the same candidate: 179 did and 0 did not. A difference there would be a call that came back differently, and no call was made again.

The judge answered the same 2400 call(s) in both columns, so nothing here is the judge changing its mind. What changed is what the harness does with those answers: it now returns a candidate on 389 of 400 run(s) where it returned one on 183, and 10 run(s) it settled without asking at all. So `human kappa` above is the agreement of the **whole selector** with the people rather than of the judge, and it is computed over a different set of decisions on the same items.

The `decided-only` row is where that shows most sharply: the denominator went from 70 item(s) to 130, because the items it used to drop are the ones the judge could not part and the rule now parts for it. A coefficient over the hard items included is not comparable with one over the easy items alone, and the movement from 0.763 to 0.480 is the price of deciding them rather than a judge that got worse.

The row that is still the judge alone is the judge-decided one: 0.278 over the 195 item(s) it was asked about, against 0.281 over 200. The calls are the same calls, so the judge's own validity had little room to move, and what it moved by is the 5 item(s) the candidates settled between themselves. That is the number to compare with 2026-09-05 when the question is the judge and not the harness.
